# One-star review candidates (draft pass)

Drafted across different essentials, in entitled-review voice, then
scored against the judge's criteria: unmistakably a one-star review
of an everyday essential, sharpest true grievance, deadpan (no
winking, no exclamation marks), a turn in the last few words, and
tight and specific with only one joke.

1. Gravity: "One star for gravity. It catches every phone, plate and
   cup I have ever dropped, instantly and at full speed, yet it has
   never once caught me." -- specific objects, instant service for
   things, none for the reviewer; the turn is the last two words.
2. Oxygen: "One star for oxygen. Free my whole life, completely
   tasteless, invisible when I need to check the level, and I still
   have to do all the breathing myself." -- good turn ("myself"),
   but it carries two jokes (tasteless, DIY breathing).
3. Sleep: "One star for sleep. Takes eight hours every night,
   cancels itself for one 3 a.m. worry, and charges me the whole
   next day for a service I never received." -- relatable, but long
   and the billing metaphor crowds the complaint.
4. The sun: "One star for the sun. Blinds me driving east in the
   morning, blinds me driving west in the evening, and is
   unavailable for most of winter." -- clean double grievance, turn
   is decent but expected.
5. Water: "One star for water. Leaks from every pipe in my house,
   ruins every phone it touches, and still expects eight glasses a
   day from me, like I am a houseplant." -- funny image, two jokes.
6. Mondays: "One star for Mondays. Arrives every week without being
   invited, ignores every cancellation request, and is somehow
   always scheduled immediately after Sunday." -- the last clause is
   stating the obvious rather than turning.
7. Time: "One star for time. Moves slowest in waiting rooms,
   fastest on holiday, and has never once paused while I looked for
   my keys." -- sharp and recognisable; turn is good, not great.
8. Memory: "One star for memory. Remembers every embarrassing thing
   I said in 2014 in full detail, and cannot recall why I walked
   into this room." -- strong runner-up; very specific, clean turn.
9. Air conditioning is not essential enough; skipped in favour of
   air itself: "One star for air. Completely transparent about
   nothing, smells of whatever it last passed, and leaves the room
   the moment I open a window in winter." -- muddled grievance.
10. Food: "One star for hunger. Returns three times a day, every
    day, with no unsubscribe option, and charges more every year
    for the same sandwich." -- modern complaint voice, solid turn.
11. Electricity: "One star for electricity. Invisible, silent, and
    only ever noticed the one evening a year it decides not to
    turn up, taking the fridge with it." -- nice, but the essential
    is a utility rather than a fact of life.
12. Gravity, second angle: "One star for gravity. Has held me down
    my entire life, charges nothing, offers no loyalty discount,
    and gets noticeably stronger on stairs." -- amusing, but the
    stairs joke is a second joke and softer than candidate 1.

## Pick

Candidate 1, gravity. It is the tightest single joke: a real,
instantly recognised grievance (gravity is flawless at dropping
your things, useless at saving you), filed deadpan like a genuine
service complaint, and the whole review turns on the final two
words, "caught me". Candidate 8 (memory) was the closest rival;
its grievance is about a faculty rather than an essential service,
and its turn lands earlier in the line.

## Refinement (second pass)

The winner was tightened before shipping: "cup" became "mug" (the
more breakable, more specific object), the trailing "yet it has"
clause was cut, and the complaint was split into two flat sentences
so it reads as an earnest service report right up to the sting.
Final text (135 characters):

"One star for gravity. It catches every phone, plate and mug I
have ever dropped, instantly, at full speed. It has never once
caught me."
